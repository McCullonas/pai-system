# Image rights, trade marks and taste

This artwork is sold commercially. Depicting a protected building, sculpture or trade mark on a product for sale is a different matter from photographing it on holiday, and several European countries actively enforce it. Check this file before generating.

These rules apply to **all three masters equally**. A restricted landmark must not appear as the panorama's hero, and above all must not be the badge motif — a badge reduces a destination to one silhouette, so it is the variant most likely to be an unmistakable reproduction of a single protected building.

None of this is legal advice. It is a working checklist that keeps the obvious traps out of the range.

---

## 1. Destinations not to produce

Four destinations should not be made at all. Offer the substitute instead.

| Destination | Why | Make instead |
|---|---|---|
| **Chambord**, France | A classified *domaine national* under Code du patrimoine L621-42. Commercial use of the château's image requires the estate's authorisation and a fee, regardless of the building's age. | Chenonceau, Amboise or Azay-le-Rideau |
| **Portmeirion**, Wales | A private village and a registered trade mark. Commercial imagery requires a licence. | Porthmadog harbour, or the Ffestiniog Railway |
| **Oświęcim**, Poland | The destination is the Auschwitz-Birkenau memorial and death camp. A decorative travel poster of a genocide site is indefensible. This is a matter of taste, not law. | Kraków or Warszawa for the same traveller |
| **Terezín**, Czechia | The fortress town was the Theresienstadt ghetto and transit camp. Same reasoning. | Litoměřice's baroque square |

The same taste rule applies generally: **no camps, massacre sites, mass graves or disaster sites**, however visually striking. War cemeteries and memorials are a narrower case — a poster of Ypres is legitimate if it depicts the Cloth Hall and the town, not the Menin Gate or the graves.

## 3. Country-level rules

**Freedom of panorama** is the right to depict a building or artwork permanently visible from a public place. Where it exists for commercial use, historic architecture is safe. Where it does not, even a street view of a modern building can infringe.

**Commercial freedom of panorama exists:** United Kingdom, Ireland, Germany, Austria, Switzerland, Spain, Portugal, Netherlands, Norway, Poland, Czechia, Hungary, Croatia.

**It does not, or is materially restricted:** **France, Italy, Greece, Belgium, Slovenia, Slovakia, Denmark (artworks), Sweden (commercial reproduction of artworks), Finland (artworks), Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania.**

Three countries go further and assert control over images of heritage they own, independently of copyright:

- **France** — *domaines nationaux* (Code du patrimoine L621-42, decree 2017-720) licence the commercial image of Chambord, the Louvre and Tuileries domain, Château d'Angers, the Palais du Rhin, the Domaine de Pau and the Élysée. Versailles is not on the list.
- **Italy** — the Codice dei beni culturali (arts. 106–108) requires a state concession and fee for commercial reproduction of state-owned cultural property. Italian courts have explicitly rejected a freedom-of-panorama defence for building exteriors (the Teatro Massimo case), and have enforced it over Michelangelo's David and the Vitruvian Man.
- **Greece** — Law 4858/2021 art. 46 requires Ministry of Culture permission and a fee payable to ODAP to reproduce monuments commercially. Greece sued Adidas over an Acropolis drone show in 2025.

There is a real argument that a stylised 1930s-poster illustration is a creative interpretation rather than a "depiction" of a monument. It is an argument, not a safe harbour.

## 4. The working heuristic

When the subject is not on any list above:

- **Completed before 1900** — almost certainly clear. Historic architecture and landscape are the safe ground.
- **Completed 1900–1955** — check the architect's death date. Protection runs 70 years from it.
- **Completed after 1955** — treat as restricted unless verified. Compose it out.
- **Landscape, coastline, mountains, water, vegetation, harbours, streets, rooftops** — always clear.
- **Sculpture, monuments, murals, lighting displays, bridges of recent design, branded buildings** — always check.

The pattern this produces is convenient: **landscape, silhouette and waterfront compositions are almost always clean**, and they are also the strongest compositions for this genre. Composing for rights and composing for beauty pull in the same direction.

## 5. Trade marks separate from copyright

Copyright is not the only exposure. Avoid rendering, and never letter onto the artwork:

- Company liveries and logos — ferry funnels, railway liveries, airline tails, brewery marks.
- Named railway journeys and their branding — *Glacier Express*, *Bernina Express*, *Jungfraujoch – Top of Europe*. Depict the train unbranded, or title the poster after the pass or valley.
- Event names — *Tour de France*, *The Open*, *Oktoberfest*, *Palio*, *TT*, *Montreux Jazz*.
- Place names that are themselves registered marks in souvenir classes — *Neuschwanstein*, *St Moritz*, *Portmeirion*.
- Protected designations — champagne house names, château names, wine appellations.
- Film and franchise associations — the Glenfinnan viaduct is fine, the Hogwarts Express is not; Skellig Michael is fine, anything Star Wars is not.

## 6. Private property and permits

Some sites restrict commercial imagery through ownership rather than IP. Compose from a public road, path or foreshore, and say so in the Source check.

- **National Trust** properties (St Michael's Mount, Lindisfarne Castle, Arlington Row, the Giant's Causeway visitor site) — commercial photography is restricted on their land; public rights of way and foreshore are not.
- **Bavarian Palace Administration** — written permission and a fee for outdoor as well as indoor shoots at its properties, including the classic Marienbrücke viewpoint.
- **Private estates and marinas** — Eilean Donan, Bamburgh, Puerto Banús, Isola Bella, the Ravello villas.
- **Managed natural sites** — Plitvice, Wieliczka.

For an illustration rather than a photograph the exposure is lower, but the safe composition is the same one: the view from public ground.

---

## 7. What to do when a rule bites

Do not refuse the destination and stop. Compose around the restriction, generate the artwork, and report the decision in one line of the Source check:

> **Rights:** Atomium excluded (SABAM enforces the Waterkeyn estate's rights); composed on the Grand-Place instead.

Only the four destinations in section 1 are refused outright, and each has a substitute to offer.
